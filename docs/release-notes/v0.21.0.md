## EML v0.21.0

Work together in one brain: a space can now be shared with other people over a relay. Your agent gets a brain of its own that goes with it into every space. Settings is reorganised around spaces, agents and your account. Brain schema 21: on the first start every brain is backed up and migrated, and your agent's identity moves into its own brain (see "What happens at the first start").

### What you get

- **Shared spaces.** A shared space is a brain that several people read and write, each with their own cockpit. Every write goes through a relay, which passes it on to every member, so everyone has the same brain. Under Settings › Spaces you create a shared space or join one with an invite link. The owner confirms each join after comparing a short fingerprint with the person over another channel. Without a connection to the relay a shared space stays readable, and writing waits until the connection is back. A project can be moved from a local space into a shared one from its page in the Brain tab: the dialog shows what moves before anything happens, and a backup is taken first.
- **EML accounts (beta).** Shared spaces need an EML account. You sign in under Settings › Account with your email and a code sent by mail. Accounts are invite-only during the beta; local spaces work as before without one.
- **Invite links carry the space's name.** The link alone is enough to join; you no longer type a name. If you already have a space with that name, the new one gets a number.
- **Your agent has its own brain.** Your agent's profile, its rules, its learnings and your profile now live in the agent's brain, not in a space. The agent brings them into every space it works in, local or shared, and they do not end up in a brain you share with others. Every rule belongs to the agent or to a project; global and personal scopes hold no rules any more. Settings › Agents shows the agent's persona, rules and the people it works with.
- **Settings, reworked.** Every tab has a heading. Spaces (was Brain) lists your local and shared spaces with their colours. Each space has its own details, and a shared space shows its members, invites and connection state. Creating, joining and deleting spaces moved here from the sidebar menu, which now only switches spaces. Deleting a local space asks for its name and takes a backup first. The cockpit's folders moved to Data, and About shows the versions and the license.
- **Tasks for more than one person.** A task can be assigned to several people or agents (`assignee_ids`, a list, replacing the single `assignee_id`). In a shared space only members and their agents can be assigned, and an unknown name is refused with the list of names that can be.
- **Move engrams within a brain** (from 0.20.10): the server route `POST /rescope` now also works in a shared space, through the relay.
- **The update banner per space.** Each space shows its own brain's migration, and a space whose brain failed to update is marked in the space switch.

### What changed for agents

- In a shared space the agent sees a short block at the start of the session: who the members are, which agents work for them, and how to assign work.
- `eml_create_task` and `eml_track_task` take `assignee_ids` (a list); `eml_list` filters with `assignee` on any of them. `eml_read_object object=user|agent` lists a shared space's members and their agents.
- `eml_rule` writes to `agent.<id>` or `project.<id>`, and `eml_learning` only to `agent.<id>`. Both go to the agent's brain and follow it into every space. A rule or learning written before v0.21 in another scope stays readable and editable where it is; the brain hygiene check names it so it can be moved.
- `eml_boot` no longer takes an `agent` parameter: a session reads only its own agent's brain.
- Sessions in Claude Code reach the brain through one endpoint in the cockpit instead of a process per session. Nothing changes in how the tools are used.

### What happens at the first start

1. **Backups.** Each brain is copied to `backups/pre-migration/` before it changes.
2. **Schema 21.** Each brain gets a log of its writes, which a shared brain needs and a local one uses to check itself. Task assignees become a list. No engram changes its content, and the migration checks that every row is still there.
3. **Your agent's brain.** The cockpit gives each agent its own brain and moves its profile, rules and learnings there, together with your profile. Rules and learnings that sat in global or in your personal scope go to the agent. Links between them and your projects stay, now pointing across brains. A dry run on copies comes first, the real run only when every check passed, with a backup in `backups/agent-upgrade/`. If the upgrade cannot run, the home stays as it was and the space says why.
4. **Brain hygiene.** What the agent brain did not take over automatically, such as a learning in a project scope, shows up in the brain hygiene banner to sort out with your agent.

Going back to 0.20.10 needs the brain backup from before the update; the cockpit's rollback says so instead of starting an older version on a newer brain.

### Tested

- All package tests pass on Linux, macOS and Windows in CI.
- The migration was audited on fresh copies of a production home (1648 and 44 engrams): every row kept, the write log checks out against the brain, integrity and foreign keys clean.
- The agent upgrade ran on a copy of a production home on a dev cockpit.
- Shared spaces: inviting and joining with the link alone were tested with two dev cockpits over the relay. Moving a project into a shared space was tested end to end against a local relay.
- Benchmark: the questions were updated for v0.21 (the sample brain's rules now belong to the agent), and 0.20.10 was measured again on them for comparison. Retrieval is unchanged against 0.20.10 (recall@5 0.946, MRR 0.892, coverage 0.973, stale rate 0.100). What an agent reads at the start grows by about 450 characters in the session start and about 1,100 characters in the tool descriptions. Agent stage: pending.

### Updating

The cockpit offers the update in its banner and under Settings › About › Updates, or run `scripts/install-app.sh 0.21.0`.
